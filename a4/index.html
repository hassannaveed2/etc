Chairman’s foreword.

“A fair test opens a door. Our responsibility is to ensure that it opens on the strength of merit.”
Prof. Dr Niaz Ahmed AkhtarChairman, Higher Education Commission of Pakistan

The Education Testing Council (ETC), established under the auspices of the Higher Education Commission (HEC), plays an important role in advancing fair, reliable and transparent assessments in Pakistan. Its work supports merit-based access to education, scholarships, employment and professional opportunities.

For a candidate, a single test result can influence the course of a life. We must therefore treat every assessment as a public trust. Candidates should be judged on their knowledge and ability, regardless of their background or connections. Consistent procedures, secure examinations and sound test design are essential to upholding that trust.

ETC has developed into a national assessment platform serving universities, government departments, regulatory bodies and professional councils. The confidence placed in ETC by these institutions brings with it a responsibility to maintain the integrity of every assessment it conducts.

As we look ahead, HEC will support ETC in improving the quality of its questions, strengthening examination security and communicating more clearly with candidates. Continued investment in technology, research and skilled people will help ETC respond to changing needs while maintaining the reliability, validity and fairness of its assessments.

I commend the ETC team and its partner institutions for their work. Together, we can build a culture in which opportunity follows merit and every candidate can have confidence in the result they receive.

Leadership navigation.

“Building confidence in assessment by placing merit, integrity and the candidate experience at the heart of everything we do.”
Dr Muhammad Imtiaz HaiderChief Executive Officer, Education Testing Council

Chief Executive Officer · Education Testing Council

At ETC, we hold ourselves to one simple principle: every candidate deserves a test that is fair, transparent, secure and consistent. Whether a young person is applying to a law college, a scholarship holder is entering a PhD programme, or a professional is seeking a licence, they should be able to trust that the result reflects their own ability.

To make this possible, we have built our work on professional standards and operational discipline. Each question in our item bank is written by trained subject experts, reviewed by several committees, tried out on real candidates and analysed statistically before it is used to make decisions. After every live test, we study the results again, so that weak or unclear questions are corrected or removed. This cycle of checking and improving is what gives our scores their meaning.

Technology is an important partner in this effort. We use automated paper generation from a calibrated item bank, optical scanning for fast and accurate marking, and online services for registration and results. As we move forward, we plan a gradual shift to computer-based testing, stronger identity verification and smarter monitoring, always with careful safeguards for data protection and confidentiality.

Our responsibility does not end with conducting examinations. We work closely with institutions to help them identify talent and make sound decisions. We listen to candidates, answer their concerns and treat complaints seriously. We believe a testing body must earn trust through its actions: open processes, accountable governance, responsive service and a culture of quality.

I thank our Board of Governors, the Higher Education Commission, our subject experts and the entire ETC team for their commitment. Together, we will continue to grow ETC into an institution that Pakistan can rely on for fair and meaningful assessment.

ETC, mandate and direction.

A national assessment platform

The Education Testing Council is Pakistan’s public-sector national testing body, working under the Higher Education Commission. It provides standardized, centralized and secure assessments for education and professional opportunities.

The Council supports candidates, universities, scholarship programmes, government departments and regulatory bodies. Its work brings test design, item development, secure delivery, scoring and reporting into one assessment system.

Our record in numbers

2 millionCandidates tested since 2017
203 centres · 40 citiesNational network, with capacity of about 100,000 candidates in a shift
251,685Calibrated items in a continuously updated bank
261 projects · 1,750 papersTests delivered for clients, with distinct papers and no repetition
850 blueprints · 802 batteriesBlueprints developed and test batteries piloted
530 subject expertsExpertise available across subject areas

Our vision

To empower future generations of students to reach their full potential in service of the nation, by building a transparent, merit-based and equitable system of education and professional opportunity, with an unwavering commitment to quality, fairness and excellence.

Our mission

To advance quality and equity in education, public service and the professions across Pakistan, by making sound decisions possible through assessments built on the core values of quality, integrity and equal opportunity.

To use the best available technology, and to develop our own tools, so that the true potential of our young people can be recognized and put to work for the country.

LAT · Law Admission Test

LAT is mandatory for admission to five-year LLB programmes in public and private universities and law colleges, following the Supreme Court’s directions on legal education reform. It assesses reasoning, language, general knowledge and written expression. Students completing intermediate / HSSC or equivalent take the test; it is held twice a year. The listed fee is Rs. 2,000.

LAW-GAT · Law Graduate Assessment Test

LAW-GAT assesses core legal knowledge and its application for LLB graduates seeking enrolment with a provincial Bar Council. A pass result is required for enrolment as an advocate. It is held quarterly across Pakistan; the listed fee is Rs. 3,000. Accuracy around the passing score is particularly important.

One commitment across products

Each test has a defined purpose, blueprint, expert review, secure administration and standardized scoring. Piloting and analysis support reliable, valid and fair selection decisions.

Postgraduate assessments.

HAT-General

The Higher Education Aptitude Test supports postgraduate study at MS and MPhil level and HEC scholarship eligibility. Five categories group candidates by broad field so that assessment takes place in a context close to their discipline.

Who should take itGraduates holding a 16-year degree or equivalent seeking MS/MPhil study.
What it measuresVerbal, quantitative and analytical reasoning.
How results are usedUniversities and HEC use scores for eligibility and ranking for admission or scholarships.
Listed feeRs. 2,000

HAAT-Subject

The Higher Education Aptitude & Achievement Test is designed for candidates moving towards doctoral study. It combines general aptitude with achievement in the candidate’s subject.

Who should take itHolders of an MS/MPhil or equivalent 18-year qualification applying for PhD programmes.
What it measuresAdvanced reasoning and in-depth subject knowledge, reviewed by subject committees.
How results are usedEligibility and merit decisions for PhD admissions and HEC scholarships.
Listed feeRs. 3,000

Relevant content, comparable decisions

The two products serve different academic stages. General aptitude supports readiness for postgraduate study; subject achievement adds evidence about preparation for doctoral work. Institutions use the relevant assessment within their published admission and scholarship rules.

Undergraduate admission testing.

USAT: common core and subject knowledge

USAT is a standardized test for undergraduate admissions and HEC undergraduate scholarships. Six categories match candidates’ intermediate streams, combining a common aptitude core with discipline-specific knowledge.

Part I comprises 75 MCQs: 20 Verbal Reasoning, 25 Quantitative Reasoning and 30 discipline-specific questions. Part II carries 25 marks: an argument-based essay (15) and a narrative essay (10), each chosen from three options.

Six categories

USAT-E · Pre-engineeringPhysics 10 · Chemistry 10 · Mathematics 10
USAT-M · Pre-medicalPhysics 8 · Chemistry 8 · Biology 14
USAT-CS · Computer sciencePhysics 8 · Computer Science 14 · Mathematics 8
USAT-GS · General scienceMathematics 10 · Statistics 10 · Economics 10
USAT-COM · CommerceAccounting 10 · Commerce 10 · Economics 10
USAT-A · Arts and humanitiesIslamiat/Ethics 10 · Pakistan Studies 10 · General Knowledge 10

What the test assesses

The discipline component covers foundational concepts, subject knowledge and applied problem-solving. Verbal and quantitative reasoning assess general cognitive aptitude. The essays assess organization, argument, expression and written communication.

The Table of Specifications balances content with thinking levels, using Bloom’s Taxonomy from knowledge and understanding through application and analysis. This makes the intended coverage explicit before the paper is assembled.

From a brief to a test.

Customized assessment

Beyond its national products, ETC builds tests for government departments, regulators, universities and projects. These include recruitment, specific cadres, admissions and screening. Every assignment starts with the selection purpose and the knowledge or skills the client needs to assess.

A controlled development process

1–3 · PlanDefine requirements, agree the blueprint and Table of Specifications, and identify the content and thinking levels.
4–6 · DevelopCommission items, review them with subject and technical experts, and check language, clarity and fairness.
7–9 · ValidatePilot questions, analyse their statistical properties, and revise or replace weak items.
10–11 · AssembleSelect calibrated items to match the blueprint, generate the paper and complete final quality checks.
12–13 · Deliver and reviewAdminister securely, score and report results, then analyse live-test evidence to improve the bank.

Scoring and reporting

MCQs are processed through standardized scoring; written responses use scoring rubrics and checking procedures. Client reporting presents the results and supporting analysis needed for the agreed selection purpose.

The feedback loop continues after delivery. Psychometric and subject experts examine item performance and document whether questions should be retained, revised or critically reviewed.

Same standard, every time

A customized test follows the same blueprint, review, piloting and analysis principles as the national products. The format can change with the purpose; the commitment to quality and fairness remains.

Blueprints, items and the bank.

Start with the blueprint

A blueprint, or Table of Specifications, defines what a test will cover, how many questions each area receives and which thinking levels are required. It links the purpose of the assessment to the questions that appear in the paper.

Bloom’s Taxonomy helps balance knowledge, understanding, application, analysis, evaluation and creation. The blueprint prevents a paper from becoming an accidental collection of available questions.

Good practice in item writing

A clear taskUse plain language, a focused question and sufficient information.
A defensible answerMake the key correct and the distractors plausible.
Relevant contentMatch the syllabus, intended skill and thinking level.
Fair contextAvoid bias, stereotypes, unnecessary complexity and clues to the answer.
Expert reviewCheck content, technical quality, language and fairness before use.

The ETC item bank

The item bank is a secure academic resource containing 251,685 calibrated test items. It allows the Council to assemble different but equivalent papers, avoid repeating questions and respond to client requirements.

Items are organized by subject and linked to their content area, thinking level and statistical properties. The twelve listed subject banks together hold 188,310 items; the overall total covers all banks. Calibration, tagging and continued review keep the resource useful as it grows.

Calibration, analysis and security.

Calibration and piloting

Calibration measures item difficulty and other properties so questions can be placed on a common scale. Items are tagged by subject, topic, thinking level and review history. Piloting provides evidence before an item is used for decisions.

Security of the bank

AccessRestrict access to authorized staff and the functions their role requires.
PreparationGenerate papers and handle confidential materials in controlled conditions.
TraceabilityRecord selection, review and use; protect data throughout delivery and scoring.

Item-level analysis

DifficultyThe p-value is the proportion answering correctly; judge it against the intended test difficulty.
DiscriminationUse corrected item–total correlation and upper–lower comparisons to examine separation of stronger and weaker candidates.
DistractorsReview option choices; options selected by fewer than 5% are non-functioning.
Rasch fitThe preferred Infit / Outfit mean-square range is 0.7–1.3.
Response patternsInvestigate blanks, invalid responses and possible miskeys.

Fairness and post-test review

DIF checks whether equally able candidates from different groups perform differently on an item. Mantel–Haenszel A/B/C classifications guide screening; category C items receive independent review. After live delivery, psychometric and subject experts review unexpected patterns and decide whether items should be retained, revised or critically reviewed.

The item life cycle and measurement.

An ongoing improvement cycle

Develop and reviewWrite items to the blueprint; check content, language, technical quality and fairness.
Pilot and calibrateGather responses, analyse performance and establish item properties.
Store and selectTag approved items and assemble papers to the required specification.
Administer and analyseDeliver securely and review live-test evidence.
Retain, revise or reviewDocument decisions, improve weak items and test revisions before reuse.

The psychometric report

After every test, ETC prepares a report on candidate results and item performance. It asks whether the paper measured ability accurately, consistently and fairly, and how each item contributed to that measurement.

Classical Test Theory works with observed scores. The Rasch / one-parameter logistic (1PL) model places candidate ability and item difficulty on a common scale measured in logits. The probability of success depends on the gap between ability and difficulty.

Targeting and separation

A person–item, or Wright, map plots abilities and item difficulties together. Gaps show where coverage is too easy, too hard or thin. Reliability and separation show how consistently a test measures and how well it distinguishes ability levels.

The report combines these measures with item-level review so that improvements can be directed to the parts of the test where they are most useful.

Precision and review decisions

The Test Information Function and Conditional SEM show where a test measures most precisely and where uncertainty is greater. Item characteristic curves and Wright maps help assess difficulty and targeting.

Item review considers discrimination, distractor functioning, response patterns and Rasch fit (preferred Infit / Outfit range 0.7–1.3). Experts decide whether to retain, revise or critically review each item; flags do not trigger automatic deletion.

Reliability and validity.

Reliable, valid and fair decisions

Reliability concerns consistency: a candidate should receive similar results when the same ability is measured under comparable conditions. Validity concerns the evidence supporting the interpretation and intended use of those results. Fairness concerns equal opportunity to demonstrate ability.

Types of reliability

Internal consistencyKR-20 and Cronbach’s alpha examine how consistently items contribute to a test score.
Test–retestChecks score stability over time when ability is expected to remain comparable.
Parallel formsExamines consistency across different versions designed to measure the same ability.
Inter-raterChecks agreement between markers of written or practical responses.
Measurement errorSEM expresses the uncertainty around an individual score and helps avoid false precision.

Sources of validity evidence

ContentBlueprints and expert review check coverage of the intended knowledge and skills.
ConstructThe assessment should measure the intended ability rather than unrelated factors.
Criterion relationshipsRelationships with relevant external outcomes can support the intended interpretation.
Consequences and useSelection rules and score use should match the purpose for which evidence was gathered.

Interpreting the evidence

No single coefficient proves that a test is suitable for every decision. Reliability, content review, statistical analysis and the context of use must be considered together, particularly around a passing or selection score.

Fairness throughout the process

Fairness means that scores depend on the knowledge and skills being assessed, rather than unrelated personal or social factors. ETC checks content for bias, uses DIF analysis, applies standardized administration and scoring, and provides reasonable arrangements on request. Candidates can raise concerns through published support and hearing procedures.

Evidence and responsible score use.

Evidence suited to the decision

LATBlueprint and language/content review support legal-study entry; consistent scoring supports comparable admission decisions.
LAW-GATCore-law coverage, standard setting and evidence around the pass score support licensing decisions.
HAT-GeneralSection and total-score reliability, category alignment and comparability support postgraduate selection.
HAAT-SubjectSubject committees, advanced-content coverage and analysis support doctoral admission use.
USATStream-specific coverage, the common core and rubric-based essays support undergraduate selection.
Customized testsThe agreed purpose, blueprint, review and analysis support the client’s particular selection task.

Reliability, validity and fairness together

ETC examines consistency of scores, alignment with the intended knowledge and skills, and the opportunity for candidates to demonstrate ability fairly. DIF review, standardized conditions and documented scoring procedures contribute to that evidence.

Relevant statistics include KR-20 / alpha, SEM and evidence about equivalence across forms. The choice of evidence depends on the assessment and the decision being made.

Responsible interpretation

A test score is an estimate with a margin of error. Small score differences should not be given more meaning than the evidence supports. Scores should be used for the purpose for which the test was designed, alongside the institution’s published selection rules.

Passing scores and ranking decisions need particular care. Reporting should explain results clearly and avoid presenting a score as a complete description of a candidate’s ability or potential.

Quality standards and review.

Quality throughout the assessment

ETC’s quality framework covers test design, question development, expert review, secure administration, scoring and reporting. Checks occur at several stages so that responsibility for quality is shared across the assessment process.

Expert committees

Content and subject reviewChecks correctness, relevance and subject coverage against the blueprint.
Technical reviewExamines item structure, keys, distractors and assessment design.
Language reviewChecks clarity, expression and language appropriate to the candidates.
Fairness reviewLooks for bias, stereotypes and context unrelated to the ability being tested.
Final reviewConfirms paper readiness and the resolution of issues identified earlier.

Evidence before and after use

Piloting and psychometric analysis support item selection. After administration, live-response evidence supports review of the whole paper and individual questions. Problems can be referred for revision, specialist investigation or a documented scoring decision.

The framework connects subject expertise with statistical evidence: neither is treated as a substitute for the other.

Continuous improvement

Quality assurance monitors the procedures that produce a reliable result as well as the result itself. Findings feed back into item-writing guidance, training, bank maintenance and operational practice.

Assurance, security and scoring.

Independent checks at several stages

Quality Assurance monitors test operations, paper development, printing and result generation. Multi-dimensional review brings content, technical, language and fairness perspectives to the same assessment.

Monitoring before, during and after delivery connects preparation, administration and follow-up. Findings inform both immediate action and improvements to later tests.

Security from bank to result

Item and paper preparationControl access, document review and manage paper generation in a secure environment.
Printing and movementProtect confidential materials and keep their handling traceable.
At the centreApply identity checks, standardized instructions, timing and monitoring.
Collection and processingCount, seal and return answer sheets securely before scanning and scoring.
Unfair-means casesUse documented procedures and give candidates a fair hearing.

Consistent scoring

MCQs use machine processing and checking procedures. Descriptive answers are marked with rubrics so that the same standard is applied across candidates. Statistical analysis checks the performance of both items and the test.

Potential miskeys, weak items and unusual results are referred for investigation. Decisions about faulty questions should be applied consistently to affected candidates.

Improving the quality system

Documented procedures, professional review and progress towards recognized quality standards support the Council’s institutional development and its commitment to trusted results.

Test operations and reach.

A coordinated national network

ETC operates through 203 test centres in 40 cities, with capacity for about 100,000 candidates in a shift. National delivery depends on coordinated planning, trained staff, secure materials and standardized instructions.

The test-day journey

RegistrationCandidates register, pay online and receive an admit card with centre, date and time.
Arrival and identityStaff verify the admit card and identity document.
Seating and instructionsCandidates follow the seating plan and receive standard instructions.
AdministrationSealed papers are opened in front of candidates and timing is observed.
Collection and scanningAnswer sheets are counted, sealed, returned securely and scanned.
ResultsScores are analysed and results are published and shared with client institutions.

Operations responsibilities

The Test Operations Department plans venues and schedules, coordinates staffing and logistics, supports candidate communication and manages secure movement of materials. Monitoring and reporting help identify and resolve issues during administration.

International administration

ETC also conducts tests abroad in cooperation with Pakistan’s embassies. Its international experience includes the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Afghanistan, Bangladesh and Sri Lanka.

Assessment glossary.

Blueprint / Table of Specifications (TOS)

The plan of a test: which topics, how many questions, and at which thinking levels.

Bloom’s Taxonomy

A way of classifying thinking from simple recall up to creating new ideas.

Item

A single test question, together with its answer options and key.

Item bank

A secure database of reviewed and calibrated items, with their statistics.

Calibration

Measuring an item’s difficulty and other properties so it can be placed on a common scale.

Pre-test / piloting

Trying out new items on a sample of students before using them for real decisions.

Post-hoc analysis

Statistical review of items and the whole test after a live administration.

Difficulty index (p-value)

The proportion of candidates who answer an item correctly.

Discrimination index

How well an item separates stronger from weaker candidates.

Distractor

A wrong answer option in an MCQ.

DIF

Differential Item Functioning: a check of whether equally able candidates from different groups perform differently on an item.

Reliability

The consistency of test scores.

INSTITUTIONAL PROFILE · 29

Assessment glossary — continued.

KR-20 / Cronbach’s alpha

Statistics that measure the internal consistency (reliability) of a test.

SEM

Standard Error of Measurement: the likely margin of error around an individual score.

Validity

The degree to which evidence supports the meaning and use of test scores.

Equating

A statistical method that makes scores on different versions of a test comparable.

Rubric

A fixed scoring guide used to mark written or practical answers consistently.

OMR

Optical Mark Recognition: machine scanning of answer sheets.

CBT

Computer-Based Testing.

IRT

Item Response Theory: a modern statistical model for analysing and calibrating items.

Rasch / 1PL model

An IRT model in which the chance of a correct answer depends only on the gap between candidate ability and item difficulty.

Logit

The unit of the Rasch scale on which both ability and difficulty are measured.

Infit / Outfit

Rasch fit statistics showing whether responses to an item follow the expected pattern (preferred range 0.7 – 1.3).

Wright map

A chart that places candidate abilities and item difficulties side by side to show targeting.

Fairness

Equal opportunity for every candidate to show their ability, with scores free of factors unrelated to the test’s purpose.
