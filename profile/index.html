Section 01 · Chairman’s message

Every door opens
for the right reason.

Merit · Transparency · Fairness · Quality

It gives me great pleasure to present the institutional profile of the Education Testing Council (ETC). ETC was created with one simple belief: every young person in Pakistan deserves an equal chance to show what they know and what they can do. A well-made test is one of the fairest tools a nation has for opening doors to education, scholarships, jobs and professions.

For many students, a single test result can shape the next chapter of their life. That is why we treat every assessment as a serious public trust. Our tests must measure ability and effort, not background, connections or luck. To achieve this, ETC follows standardized procedures, secure examination practices and international good practice in test design, so that the same rules apply to every candidate, in every city.

Over the past few years, ETC has grown into a national assessment platform. It now serves universities, government departments, regulatory bodies and professional councils across the country. Each new partnership reflects growing confidence in our methods, and each one reminds us that confidence must be earned again with every test we deliver.

Looking ahead, our priorities are clear. We will keep strengthening the quality of our questions, the security of our operations and the clarity of our communication with candidates. We will invest in technology, research and people, so that our assessments remain reliable, valid and fair as the needs of the country change.

I am grateful to the Higher Education Commission, the members of the Board of Governors, our partner institutions and the dedicated ETC team for their support. I invite all stakeholders to join us in building a culture where opportunity follows merit, and where every candidate can trust the result they receive.

Section 02 · Executive Director

Strengthening assessment.

Message from the Executive Director, Higher Education Commission

The Higher Education Commission established the Education Testing Council to give Pakistan a credible, professionally managed and self-sustaining national testing body. Our aim is straightforward: to make sure that access to higher education and professional opportunities is decided by merit, through processes that are open, consistent and trusted.

Assessment in the modern world is changing quickly. Institutions need tests that are standardized, secure and quick to report, while candidates need clear information and fair treatment. ETC responds to these needs by combining sound measurement methods with appropriate technology. Every stage, from writing a question to publishing a result, is guided by evidence and checked for quality.

A central priority is public and institutional confidence. This depends on good governance, clear procedures, secure examination arrangements and timely results. ETC will continue to build its professional capacity so that every assessment is delivered with impartiality and accountability.

Looking forward, ETC will expand its support for admissions, scholarships, recruitment and professional licensing. It will deepen collaboration with universities, public-sector organizations, employers and professional bodies. Through careful use of digital tools, the Council will make assessment more accessible, efficient and responsive to the people it serves.

In the end, ETC’s work is about Pakistan’s human capital. By measuring knowledge, skills and potential fairly, it helps the right people reach the right opportunities, and strengthens the culture of merit across our education system.

Section 03 · Chief Executive Officer

Building confidence
in assessment.

Message from the Chief Executive Officer, Education Testing Council

At ETC, we hold ourselves to one simple principle: every candidate deserves a test that is fair, transparent, secure and consistent. Whether a young person is applying to a law college, a scholarship holder is entering a PhD programme, or a professional is seeking a licence, they should be able to trust that the result reflects their own ability.

To make this possible, we have built our work on professional standards and operational discipline. Each question in our item bank is written by trained subject experts, reviewed by several committees, tried out on real candidates and analysed statistically before it is used to make decisions. After every live test, we study the results again, so that weak or unclear questions are corrected or removed. This cycle of checking and improving is what gives our scores their meaning.

Technology is an important partner in this effort. We use automated paper generation from a calibrated item bank, optical scanning for fast and accurate marking, and online services for registration and results. As we move forward, we plan a gradual shift to computer-based testing, stronger identity verification and smarter monitoring, always with careful safeguards for data protection and confidentiality.

Our responsibility does not end with conducting examinations. We work closely with institutions to help them identify talent and make sound decisions. We listen to candidates, answer their concerns and treat complaints seriously. We believe a testing body must earn trust through its actions: open processes, accountable governance, responsive service and a culture of quality.

I thank our Board of Governors, the Higher Education Commission, our subject experts and the entire ETC team for their commitment. Together, we will continue to grow ETC into an institution that Pakistan can rely on for fair and meaningful assessment.

05 Section 05 · About the Council

About the Education
Testing Council.

Our background, institutional journey and legal mandate

5.1Background

The Education Testing Council (ETC) is a public-sector testing body set up under the Higher Education Commission (HEC) Ordinance. It was first conceived in 2017. After reviewing how the Council should operate, the Commission approved a revamped ETC in its 34th meeting, held on 30–31 August 2018, as an autonomous and self-sustaining entity. ETC was formally notified in The Gazette of Pakistan on 15 June 2022.

ETC offers standardized and centralized testing based on international best practice. This means that tests are designed to a fixed plan, delivered under the same conditions everywhere, and scored in the same way for every candidate. It also means that results from different cities, sessions and test forms can be compared fairly.

Section 05 · Legal mandate

5.3Legal education reforms

An important part of ETC’s work comes from the reform of legal education in Pakistan. On 31 August 2018, while considering the reports of the Legal Education Reform Committees in Constitution Petition No. 134/2012 and related applications, the Supreme Court of Pakistan decided that:

Para 8Key regulatorsThe Pakistan Bar Council (PBC) and the Higher Education Commission (HEC) are the key regulators responsible for supervising and enforcing professional standards in legal education.
Para 15(1)LAW-GAT, quarterlyA Law Graduate Assessment Test (LAW-GAT) for law graduates seeking enrolment to the Bar shall be held on a quarterly basis by HEC, as the executing institution, throughout Pakistan, using a question bank prepared for the test.
Para 15(2)LAT, twice a yearA Law Admission Test (LAT) for all law colleges in Pakistan shall be held twice a year by HEC.

ETC carries out these responsibilities on behalf of HEC. As a result, LAT and LAW-GAT are now national gateways to legal education and to the legal profession.

In simple terms

ETC is HEC’s own testing arm. It is publicly owned, run as an autonomous and self-financing body, and legally responsible for key national tests such as LAT and LAW-GAT.

Section 09 · Product 9.2
LAT

Law Admission Test

Fee*Rs. 2,000
HeldTwice a year
ForLLB applicants

LAT is the national entry test for the five-year LLB programme. Following the Supreme Court’s decision on legal education reforms, it is mandatory for admission to LLB programmes in all public and private universities and law colleges in Pakistan, and is held twice a year.

Purpose
To make sure that only students with the basic reasoning, language and general knowledge needed for legal study enter law programmes.
Who should take it
Students who have completed, or are completing, intermediate (HSSC) or equivalent and wish to study law.
What it measures
Reading and comprehension in English, logical and analytical reasoning, general knowledge and written expression, as announced in the official test pattern.
How results are used
Universities use the LAT result as a required eligibility condition in their LLB admission process.
Frequency
Twice a year (bi-annual), as directed by the Supreme Court.

Section 09 · Product 9.3
LAW-GAT

Law Graduate Assessment Test

Fee*Rs. 3,000
HeldQuarterly
ForLLB graduates

LAW-GAT is a licensing-type test. It is mandatory for law graduates who wish to enrol as Advocates in any Bar Council. It checks that new lawyers have the core legal knowledge needed to serve the public responsibly.

Purpose
To confirm that law graduates meet a minimum standard of legal knowledge before entering practice.
Who should take it
Holders of an LLB degree seeking enrolment with a provincial Bar Council.
What it measures
Knowledge and application of core areas of law, drawn from a question bank prepared specially for the test.
How results are used
A pass result is required for enrolment as an Advocate. Because it is a pass/fail decision, ETC pays special attention to accuracy around the passing score.
Frequency
Quarterly, held across Pakistan, as directed by the Supreme Court.

Section 10 · Item writing

10.5Good practice in item writing

In line with international practice, including the ETS Standards for Quality and Fairness, ETC item writers are trained to:

  1. Test one clear idea per question, linked to a specific blueprint cell.
  2. Write a clear question stem and one, and only one, correct answer.
  3. Use plausible wrong options (distractors) based on common mistakes, not tricks.
  4. Avoid unnecessary difficult language, so that the question measures the skill and not reading speed.
  5. Avoid content that could be offensive, stereotyped or unfamiliar to particular groups of candidates.

12 Section 12 · Psychometric properties of test items

Psychometric
properties.

Pre-test (pilot) and post-hoc analysis

Psychometrics is the science of measuring knowledge and ability accurately. ETC uses it twice for every item: before the item is used for real decisions (pre-test analysis) and after every live test (post-hoc analysis).

12.1Why item analysis matters

A test score is only as good as the questions behind it. A question may look fine to an expert but still behave badly with real candidates. It may be too easy, too hard, confusing, have two possible answers, or favour one group over another. Statistical item analysis finds these problems using actual candidate responses, so that they can be fixed before they affect anyone’s result.

45Psychometric properties
Section 12 · Before live use

12.2Pre-test (pilot) analysis

Before an item is used in a live test, it is tried out on a sample of students who are similar to the real candidates. ETC has pilot-tested 802 test batteries so far. The pilot shows how each item really behaves.

  1. Assemble pilot forms that cover the blueprint, mixing new items with a few proven anchor items.
  2. Administer the pilot to a suitable sample of students at partner institutions under real test conditions.
  3. Score and analyse each item for difficulty, discrimination, distractor performance and fairness.
  4. Classify each item as accept, revise or reject using the criteria in the next table.
  5. Revise borderline items in the 2nd editorial review, and re-pilot them where needed.
  6. Calibrate and store accepted items in the bank with their statistics.

Section 12 · Fairness

12.4Fairness analysis (DIF)

Following the approach published by ETS (Educational Testing Service, USA), DIF analysis compares candidates from different groups (for example male and female candidates, or urban and rural candidates) who have the same overall ability. If one group still finds an item much harder, the item may contain something unrelated to the skill being measured, such as unfamiliar context or wording.

DIF categoryMeaningUse in test assembly
A — NegligibleLittle or no difference between matched groups.Preferred; used freely.
B — Slight to moderateSmall but noticeable difference.Used only when needed, choosing items with the smallest DIF.
C — Moderate to largeLarge, statistically significant difference.Reviewed by an independent fairness panel; used only if essential and judged free of bias.
A signal, not a verdict

DIF on its own does not prove bias. It is a signal that an item must be examined by trained reviewers, who decide whether the difference reflects a real skill difference or an unfair feature of the question.

Section 12 · After the live test

12.5Post-hoc analysis after the live test

Once a live test has been administered, and before results are announced, ETC runs a full post-hoc analysis on the actual candidate responses:

Key validationEvery item is checked for signs of a wrong key or two correct answers, for example when many high-scoring candidates choose the same wrong option.
Item re-analysisDifficulty, discrimination and distractor statistics are recalculated on the full population and compared with pilot values.
Fairness re-checkDIF is calculated where group sizes are large enough.
Test-level analysisScore distribution, mean, spread, reliability (KR-20 / Cronbach’s alpha) and Standard Error of Measurement are calculated.
Form comparabilityWhere several versions of a paper are used, their difficulty is compared and, if necessary, scores are statistically equated.
DecisionsAny flawed item is removed from scoring or credited to all candidates, so that no candidate is harmed. The decision is documented.

Section 13 · Reliability and validity

Reliability and
validity.

Making sure scores are consistent, accurate and meaningful

Reliability and validity are the two most important qualities of any test. The ETS Standards for Quality and Fairness describe validity as one of the most important attributes of test quality, and require that scores be reliable enough for their intended use. ETC applies the same principles.

13.1What is reliability?

Reliability means consistency. A reliable test gives similar results if the same candidate could take it again, or take a different but equivalent version. Think of a weighing scale: if it shows 60 kg, then 63 kg, then 57 kg for the same person in five minutes, it cannot be trusted. A test is the same.

Same person · five minutes

Type of reliabilityQuestion it answersHow ETC checks it

Benchmark

Section 13 · Using scores well

13.4Interpreting scores responsibly

An example of the Standard Error of Measurement

Suppose a test has a reliability of 0.90 and a score spread (standard deviation) of 10 marks. The SEM is about 3 marks:

10 × √(1 − 0.90) ≈ 3.2

This means a candidate who scores 62 would probably score between about 59 and 65 if tested again.

Our advice to institutions

ETC advises institutions not to treat very small score differences as meaningful, and to use scores together with other relevant information.
