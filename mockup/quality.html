Blueprints

Every test starts with a plan

A blueprint (Table of Specifications) makes sure the paper covers the right topics in the right proportions, and asks candidates to think at the right levels. ETC uses Bloom’s Taxonomy to describe these levels.

Good practice in item writing

  • One clear idea per question, linked to a blueprint cell.
  • A clear stem and one, and only one, correct answer.
  • Plausible distractors based on common mistakes, not tricks.
  • Plain language, so the question measures the skill and not reading speed.
  • No offensive, stereotyped or unfamiliar content for any group.

The ETC item bank

A calibrated national resource

230,685

items developed to date

The bank lets ETC build many different but equivalent papers, avoid repeating questions, and respond quickly to new client needs. Every item is tagged with its subject, Bloom’s level, difficulty, discrimination, fairness flag, usage history and review trail.

Access is strictly controlled and logged. Papers are produced by an automated system, so no single person sees a complete paper in advance.

Psychometric analysis

Every item is checked twice — before it counts, and after every test

Pre-test (pilot)

Before the item is used for real decisions · 802 test batteries piloted

  1. Assemble pilot forms covering the blueprint, with a few proven anchor items.
  2. Administer to a sample of similar students at partner institutions.
  3. Analyse difficulty, discrimination, distractors and fairness.
  4. Accept, revise or reject each item; re-pilot borderline items.
  5. Calibrate and store accepted items in the bank with their statistics.

Effect on candidates: none — pilot scores do not count.

Post-hoc

After the live test, before results are released · all candidates

  • Key validation — checks for a wrong key or two correct answers.
  • Item re-analysis — statistics recalculated on the full population.
  • Fairness re-check — DIF where group sizes allow.
  • Test-level analysis — reliability (KR-20 / alpha) and SEM.
  • Form comparability — scores equated where several versions are used.

Any flawed item is removed from scoring or credited to all candidates, so that no candidate is harmed.

Reliability

Consistency: similar results if the same candidate took an equivalent version again.

Validity

Accuracy of meaning: the score tells us what we need to know for this decision.

Interpreting scores

With reliability 0.90 and a spread of 10 marks, a score of 62 likely lies between about 59 and 65 — so very small differences should not be over-read.

Quality assurance

Multi-layer review of every question

Five quality-control committees

Academic Committee
Sets academic policy and oversees the content and standards of assessments.
Subject Committee
Reviews content within each subject for correctness, relevance and full blueprint coverage.
Paper Review Committee
Reviews assembled papers as a whole for balance, quality and suitability.
Peer Review Committee
Independent, blind review of individual questions by subject experts.
MCQ Contributors
Trained subject specialists who write new questions to ETC guidelines.

Four questions asked of every item

  • Technical

    Is the content correct, with exactly one right answer, matching the blueprint and Bloom’s level?

  • Grammatical

    Is the language correct, consistent and free of errors?

  • Linguistic

    Is the wording clear and simple, so it tests the skill and not reading difficulty?

  • Fairness

    Is it free of bias, stereotypes and material unfamiliar or offensive to any group?

An independent Quality Assurance Department

Works independently of the teams it checks, monitoring test operations, paper development, result generation and printing, and conducting unfair-means hearings.

Fairness & security

Three-tier monitoring and secure handling

  1. Tier 1

    Centre supervisors & invigilators

    Running the test correctly inside each hall.

  2. Tier 2

    ETC monitoring teams

    Visiting centres, checking compliance and reporting problems in real time.

  3. Tier 3

    Senior HEC officials

    Independent oversight of the whole process and protocol compliance.

  • Papers generated automatically in a secure environment.
  • Secure printing at ETC’s own press, with controlled access.
  • Sealed, tracked transport and storage of materials.
  • Identity checks at entry to prevent impersonation.

Looking ahead, ETC plans to seek ISO certification for its testing operations and to introduce certification programmes for item writers and reviewers.
